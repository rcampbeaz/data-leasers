# Distribution: every route to the first 20 LOIs

The target is 20 companies that have started, completed or are holding an LOI by the end of Q1 2027.
`docs/ai-marketing-engine.md` does the funnel arithmetic and the legal walls;
`docs/prospect-data-sources.md` says where the firms come from; `docs/agent-staff.md` says which
parts an agent may do. This page is the distribution side: every channel worth working, what it
actually requires, and what to do first.

## The honest starting point

Cold email alone cannot produce 20 deals in a quarter. At a 5% reply rate a 20-deal target implies
roughly 25,000 emails, which is neither deliverable nor lawful at the volume the target needs. Cold
email is a supporting channel here. The levers that can actually move 20 firms into an LOI are:

1. **Referral partners** who already sit in front of owner-managed firms and are asked about exits,
   succession or client-book value.
2. **Association and trade editorial**, where a firm's owner reads about practice economics rather
   than about data licensing.
3. **LinkedIn**, worked as a person with a point of view, not as a brand posting links.

Everything below is ordered by that.

## 1. Referral partners

A referral partner is anyone who talks to the owner of a 10-50 person professional firm about money,
succession or operations. They are worth more than any ad because they arrive with a reason to be
trusted.

**Who, in order of fit:**

- **Practice brokers.** Accounting Practice Sales, Poe Group Advisors, Legal Brokers Ltd, The
  Practice Market, Practice Orbit. Every listing is an owner who has decided to sell something. A
  broker with 40 live listings has 40 conversations a year about what a client book is worth, and no
  answer for the data the firm is sitting on.
- **M&A advisers and consolidators' deal teams.** Heartland, PDS Health, Azets, Xeinadin and similar
  buyers acquire these firms continuously. They value the client book; the data is an unbooked asset
  on the same balance sheet.
- **Accountants to the firms.** An accountant who prepares accounts for 30 small law firms knows
  which ones have a 20-year-old practice-management system and an owner near retirement.
- **Practice-management software vendors and IT support firms** serving these sectors. They know who
  is moving off an old system, which is exactly the moment the historical data becomes a decision.
- **Professional bodies and their regional branches**, as a route to editorial and events rather than
  to a list.

**The offer to a partner, in one line:** you introduce an owner; if it becomes an LOI, you are paid a
referral fee, and the owner keeps the buyer relationship and the no-broker-fee economics. The site
already states no broker fee on the Mode side, so the pitch is that the firm keeps the upside it
would otherwise lose to an intermediary.

**What the CRM already holds:** `partners` rows with identity fields, a referral status, and a
six-month deadline field. That deadline is the enforcement mechanism: a registered referral has a
date by which it must become something, or it is closed. Nothing about partner management needs new
software - it needs a partner pipeline worked weekly.

**The sequence:** identify the partner, personalise on their actual listings or client base, offer
the referral terms, agree the introduction mechanic (a warm email with the owner cc'd, or a call),
then track each introduction as a company with `source = partner name`.

## 2. Editorial and events

Owners of professional firms read about their own economics. That is the opening: an article about
what a client book is worth is read by the people who own one, and the data question follows
naturally.

- **Association newsletters and journals** for accounting, law and medical practice managers. Pitch a
  bylined piece, not a product mention: what the data inside a firm is worth, how a buyer values it,
  what confidentiality actually requires. The Mode figures are the only figures used, each with a
  link.
- **CPD and practice-management webinars**, where the audience is partners and practice managers.
  A 30-minute session on "what happens to your client data when you sell" reaches the exact buyer.
- **Podcasts for firm owners**, as a guest rather than a sponsor.
- **The site's own explainer pages** as the artefact every channel points at. The pages that name
  the `$100K-$5M` range are the ones a partner will forward, because a forwarded page has to answer
  the price question without a call.

The copy rule from the site applies everywhere: informative, no gimmicks, no figure without its
source.

## 3. LinkedIn, worked as a person

- One profile, the operator's, posting two or three times a week: what a firm's data is worth, what
  buyers ask for, what a dataset assessment looks like. The console drafts these; a person approves
  and schedules them.
- Comment on the posts of brokers, consolidators and practice-management vendors. That is where the
  partner conversations start, and it costs nothing.
- A LinkedIn newsletter for the same audience, so the articles have a home that notifies subscribers.
- No automated connection requests, no scraped lists, no AI-generated comment replies. Those
  destroy the one asset the channel depends on.

## 4. The buyer side

Mode is the counterparty, not a channel, but the buyer's own content is distribution: a firm that
reads about what buyers are paying for data arrives already qualified. Anything jointly published
needs Mode's sign-off, and the partner-confidential figures stay out of it.

## 5. Paid, only after the free channels are working

Ads are the last thing to add, not the first, because the free channels also produce the language
that makes an ad work. When they are added: search intent on "sell accounting practice client list",
"sell law firm data", "practice valuation client book" is worth more than display. Budget belongs
behind whichever page converts, not behind awareness.

## 6. What actually drives traffic

Traffic is a by-product of being useful to a specific reader. In practice:

- **One evergreen page per question an owner types**: what is my client list worth, can I sell my
  firm's data, is client data confidential after a sale, what does a buyer check. Each page answers
  it fully and offers the assessment.
- **The assessment as the conversion**, not a demo call. A firm that submits a dataset for
  assessment is further along than one that books a call, and the assessment produces the note that
  the LOI needs anyway.
- **Republish, don't multiply.** One article becomes a LinkedIn post, a newsletter section, an email
  to partners, and a section of an explainer page. The console already handles the per-channel
  variants.

## 7. Order of work

1. Partner list built from brokerages and consolidators, and the first ten conversations opened.
2. Two bylined pieces pitched to associations; one webinar booked.
3. LinkedIn cadence running from the console with a person approving.
4. Explainer pages for the four owner questions, each with the assessment as the call to action.
5. Paid search only once a page converts.

Nothing in this list needs a new tool. It needs the CRM worked weekly against the six-month referral
deadline and the 20-LOI target, and the console keeping the publishing cadence honest.
